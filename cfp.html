Call for Papers

INFOCOM 2027 Workshop on Embodied Intelligence Networks (EIN)

Full-day workshop · Monday, May 24, 2027 · Honolulu, Hawaii, USA. Short technical papers on the network architectures, protocols, algorithms, and system designs for embodied intelligence networks.

Motivation and Scope

Embodied agents, from collaborative robots and humanoids to smart glasses and industrial IoT sensors, increasingly perceive and act in the physical world, often directed by software agents that coordinate through protocols such as the Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Agent2Agent (A2A). Their perception and decisions critically depend on an efficient, low-latency, highly dependable, and adaptive network infrastructure.

The second EIN workshop is a full-day, interdisciplinary forum on the network architectures, protocols, algorithms, and system designs for embodied intelligence networks, with two highlighted themes this year: multi-agent embodied systems, and virtual and real clients in embodied AI. We welcome forward-looking research, novel system designs, and deployment experiences. Papers with open-source artifacts are especially encouraged, and the freshness of visionary ideas is more important than the completeness of theoretical proofs.

Topics of Interest

Submissions are solicited for short technical papers of up to six pages, each presented as a talk. Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Multi-agent embodied systems (highlighted): Communication, coordination, and task allocation among embodied and software agents; collective perception and multi-agent planning over the network; agent communication protocols (e.g., MCP, A2A) and quality of service for agentic traffic; semantic communication and intent-aware reasoning; networking for robot fleets, humanoids, wearables, and teleoperation.
  • Virtual and real clients in embodied AI (highlighted): Hybrid federated and distributed learning across simulated and physical clients; sim-to-real transfer over the network; emulating large populations of virtual clients alongside real devices; keeping virtual and physical state consistent; digital twins, simulators, toolchains, and testbeds.
  • Network infrastructure for embodied foundation models: Split, distributed, and streaming inference of vision-language-action (VLA) models and world models; edge computing and in-network acceleration; 6G and AI-native RAN architectures for real-time control loops; integrated sensing and communication; large-scale data collection over the network.
  • Dependable embodied networks: Trust, safety, and security for embodied agents and network infrastructure; safe behavior under network degradation; federated learning and differential privacy; measurement studies, benchmarks, and open datasets; real-world deployments in smart cities, manufacturing, and disaster relief.

Submission Guidelines

Papers must use the standard IEEE two-column format of the INFOCOM 2027 main conference and must not exceed six pages, including references.

All papers receive single-blind peer review, with at least three reviews from members of the Technical Program Committee. Conflicts of interest are enforced through EDAS, and papers co-authored by an organizer are handled by a TPC member with no conflict.

Accepted papers must be presented in person at the workshop by one of the authors and will be submitted for inclusion in IEEE Xplore.

Papers will be submitted via EDAS. The link to the submission site will be posted here once submissions open.
